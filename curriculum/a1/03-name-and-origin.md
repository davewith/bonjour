# Asking someone's name and where they are from

You can now introduce yourself. This lesson flips the conversation — you
ask the questions.

## Tu or vous?

French has two words for "you":

| | Informal | Formal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Question | Comment **t'**appelles-**tu** ? | Comment **vous** appelez-**vous** ? |
| Answer | Je m'appelle... | Je m'appelle... |

Default to **vous** with adults you don't know. If they say *"On peut se
tutoyer"* (we can use tu), switch to **tu**.

## Asking for a name

- **Comment vous appelez-vous ?** — formal, standard.
- **Comment t'appelles-tu ?** — friends, peers, children.
- Casual speech: **Tu t'appelles comment ?** (word order loosens in
  everyday French — you will hear it constantly).

## Asking where someone is from

**D'où venez-vous ?** — "Where do you come from?"

Answer with **Je viens de...**:

- Je viens de Londres. — from a city
- Je viens d'Écosse. — *de* contracts to *d'* before a vowel
- Je viens du Japon. — *du* before masculine countries with an article
- Je viens des Pays-Bas. — *des* for plural countries

Then return the question: **Et vous ?** — it is polite and keeps the
conversation going.

## Mini-dialogue to practise

> — Bonjour, comment vous appelez-vous ?
> — Je m'appelle Thomas. Enchanté.
> — Enchanté. D'où venez-vous ?
> — Je viens de Bordeaux. Et vous ?

Say it aloud with the audio — both roles, twice.
