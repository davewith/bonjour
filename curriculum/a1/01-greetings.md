# Greetings and basic expressions

Welcome to your first French lesson. French greetings change with the time
of day and with how well you know the person — but one rule keeps you safe:
**when in doubt, say "Bonjour"**.

## The three greetings

- **Bonjour** — good day / hello. Use it from morning until early evening
  with anyone: shops, strangers, teachers, friends' parents.
- **Bonsoir** — good evening. From about 6pm onwards, or when you arrive
  somewhere in the evening.
- **Salut** — hi / bye. Informal, friends only. It works both as hello
  and goodbye.

Pair **Bonjour** with a nod and a smile; French people often greet shop
staff the moment they enter. Skipping it is noticed.

## Polite formulas

Three phrases carry you through almost any encounter:

| French | English |
| --- | --- |
| Merci | Thank you |
| Merci beaucoup | Thank you very much |
| S'il vous plaît | Please (formal) |
| Pardon / Excusez-moi | Sorry / excuse me |
| Au revoir | Goodbye |

## How are you?

**Comment ça va ?** (How are you?) — the everyday answer is
**Ça va bien, merci** (Fine, thank you), then turn it back:
**Et vous ?** (And you?).

With close friends the short form **Ça va ?** replaces the full question.

## Cultural note

Greeting matters more in French than in many English-speaking countries.
A transaction without "Bonjour" and "Au revoir" feels abrupt. Two extra
words — and you sound polite immediately.

## Pronunciation targets

- **Bonjour**: the "r" is soft and low in the throat — do not roll it.
- **S'il vous plaît**: "plaît" rhymes with "pleh" — the final "t" is silent.
- **Merci**: the "c" is soft ("seh"), and the final "i" is pronounced.
