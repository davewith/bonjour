    kind: grammar
    title: J'aime... saying what you like
    summary: J'aime + noun or infinitive, negated with pas
    explanation: >-
      "Aimer" (to like) joins with "je" as "j'aime" before a vowel —
      "J'aime le café" (I like coffee). Follow it with a noun carrying
      its article ("J'aime la musique") or an infinitive ("J'aime
      voyager"). To negate, use "Je n'aime pas le vin" (written and
      careful speech) or "J'aime pas le vin" (everyday speech). Round
      out preferences with "Je préfère" (I prefer) and "Je n'aime
      jamais" (I never like).
  - id: a1-10-linking-words
    kind: grammar
    title: et, mais, parce que, donc
    summary: Join ideas inside one sentence
    explanation: >-
      "et" (and) adds, "mais" (but) contrasts, "parce que" (because)
      gives a reason, "donc" (so) gives a result — "J'aime le café,
      mais je n'aime pas le thé." They link clauses within a sentence.
      In writing, put "parce que" in the middle — starting a sentence
      with it sounds like casual speech. Frequency words join too —
      "aussi" (also), "toujours" (always), "jamais" (never).

commonMistakes:
  - wrong: Je aime le café
    right: J'aime le café
    why: Before a vowel "je" contracts to "j'" — the two words join as j'aime.
  - wrong: J'aime café
    right: J'aime le café
    why: French nouns need an article — le café, la musique, les chaussures — never a bare noun after aimer.
  - wrong: Parce que j'aime le café, je vais au bar.
    right: Je vais au bar parce que j'aime le café.
    why: In writing, state the main idea first and put parce que mid-sentence — starting with it is casual spoken style.

# Basic everyday conversation

This is your A1 capstone. You already have every piece — greetings,
introductions, numbers, directions, prices. This lesson adds the two
things that turn isolated sentences into conversation: saying what you
like, and joining ideas with linking words.

## Saying what you like

**J'aime...** (I like...) does most of the work:

- J'aime le café. — I like coffee. (noun + article)
- J'aime la musique et le cinéma. — I like music and movies.
- J'aime voyager. — I like to travel. (verb + infinitive)
- Je préfère le thé. — I prefer tea.
- Je n'aime pas le vin. — I don't like wine. (full negation)
- J'aime pas le matin. — I don't like mornings. (everyday speech)

Two details make or break the sentence: the article — **J'aime café**
is wrong, it must be **J'aime le café** — and the contraction —
**j'aime**, never "je aime".

## Linking words

| French | English | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| et | and | J'aime le café et le thé. |
| mais | but | J'aime le café, mais je n'aime pas le thé. |
| parce que | because | J'aime le week-end parce que je dors. |
| donc | so | Il pleut, donc je reste. |
| aussi | also | J'aime aussi le cinéma. |
| toujours | always | Je vais toujours au café. |
| jamais | never | Je ne bois jamais de thé. |

Put **parce que** in the middle of the sentence when you write —
starting a sentence with it is fine when you speak. **Mais** always
sets up a contrast: if the second half flips the first half, use it.

## Your capstone mini-dialogue

Everything you have learned, chained together:

> — Bonjour ! Comment ça va ?
> — Ça va bien, merci. Et vous ?
> — Bien, merci. Je m'appelle Claire. Enchantée !
> — Enchanté. Je suis de Lyon. Et vous ?
> — Je suis de Bordeaux. Vous parlez français ?
> — Un peu ! J'aime le français, mais c'est difficile.
> — Ça va ! Ça coûte combien, ce livre ?
> — Dix euros.
> — D'accord, je prends. Merci !
> — Je vous en prie.

Read it aloud twice with the audio — once as Claire, once as the other
speaker. Then swap in your own details: your name, your city, your
price, what you like.

## Next steps — A1 to A2

You can now greet people, introduce yourself, count, talk about your
age and origin, ask directions, shop, and say what you like. That is
A1.

At A2 you will meet the passé composé (talking about the past),
imperatives for giving orders, and longer stories — but every new form
will attach to the frames you already own. Keep the chunks, add the
tenses, and the conversation keeps going.

Bon courage — et à bientôt !
