# Asking for directions

Getting lost is part of travel — but in French, one polite opener and
five direction words will get you found again. This lesson teaches the
question, the directions, and the manners that go with them.

## Asking where something is

- **Où est la gare ?** — Where is the station? (one place)
- **Où est la boulangerie ?** — Where is the bakery?
- **Où sont les toilettes ?** — Where are the toilets? (plural)

**Est** for singular, **sont** for plural. That one switch keeps the
whole question correct. You already know *où* from the question-words
lesson — this is where it earns its keep.

## Direction vocabulary

| French | English | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| à gauche | to the left | Tournez à gauche. |
| à droite | to the right | C'est à droite. |
| tout droit | straight ahead | Allez tout droit. |
| près de | near | La banque est près de la gare. |
| loin de | far from | Le musée est loin d'ici. |
| la rue | the street | Traversez la rue. |
| traverser | to cross | Il faut traverser la rue. |

Keep **à** in front of *gauche* and *droite*. **Tout droit** never
changes form — it is the fixed phrase for going straight ahead.

## Stopping a stranger

Three steps, every time:

1. **Excusez-moi, madame...** / **Pardon, monsieur...**
2. **Où est la boulangerie, s'il vous plaît ?**
3. **Merci beaucoup !**

French passers-by expect the opener. A bare question is understood, but
the polite frame is what makes people stop and help you.

## A full ask-and-answer dialogue

> — Bonjour ! Excusez-moi, madame.
> — Oui ?
> — Où est la boulangerie, s'il vous plaît ?
> — Elle est à droite, après la rue. Traversez la rue, puis c'est à gauche.
> — Près de la banque ?
> — Exactement, elle est près de la banque.
> — Merci beaucoup, bonne journée !
> — Je vous en prie.

Say it aloud with the audio — both roles, twice. Then swap the places:
la gare, le musée, les toilettes.

## Pronunciation

- **droite** — one syllable "druat": the final *e* is silent and *oi*
  is a single smooth glide. Never say "droit-e".
- **gauche** — "gosh": the *ch* is soft ("sh") and the final *e* is
  silent.
- **Où** — a pure "oo" sound, held a touch longer because it opens the
  question.
- **traverser** — soft throat *r*, three syllables, tra-ver-ser.

Play the spoken examples and repeat each one twice — once slowly, once
at natural speed.
