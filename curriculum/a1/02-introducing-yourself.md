# Introducing yourself

Now you can greet people — next, tell them who you are. Two patterns do
almost all the work.

## Your name

**Je m'appelle David** — "My name is David" (literally *I call myself*).
This is the phrase to use; "Je suis David" (I am David) is understood but
less common for names.

After they give their name, respond with **Enchanté** (male speaker) or
**Enchantée** (female speaker) — "delighted to meet you".

## Who you are

**Je suis...** introduces nationality, job, or student status:

- Je suis étudiant. — I am a student.
- Je suis anglais. — I am English.
- Je travaille. — I work.

## Where you are from

**Je suis de...** + city or country:

- Je suis de Manchester. — I'm from Manchester.
- Je suis du Canada. — I'm from Canada (countries with *le/la/les* use
  *du/de la/des*).
- Je suis des États-Unis. — I'm from the USA.

## Putting it together

A complete first introduction is three lines:

1. Bonjour, je m'appelle...
2. Enchanté(e).
3. Je suis de... / J'apprends le français.

Notice how much you can say with fixed chunks — you do not need grammar
rules yet, just these frames. Repeat the spoken examples aloud.
